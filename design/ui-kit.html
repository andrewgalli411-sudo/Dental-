Verifi UI Kit

Tokens and component specs for the practice-facing morning report and the internal review queue. Built for a front desk scanning at a counter and a founder verifying eligibility — clarity and trust over decoration.

Judgment call

Accent is a desaturated steel blue, not teal or violet. It reads as institutional/clinical and, being low-chroma, never competes with the status colors — which is the whole point: when everything neutral is grey and the only saturated things on screen are status, the eye goes straight to problems.

Component

Status badge

The single most important element. Status is encoded three ways — icon shape, word, and color — so it survives colorblindness and a black-and-white printout, and reads in a half-second glance.

Clean vs. scannable

A bare colored dot would be tidier and more minimal. Rejected: a dot fails the colorblind ~8% of staff, vanishes in a B&W print, and forces the reader to recall a legend. The chip costs horizontal space and looks busier, but it self-explains at a glance. Scannability wins.

Clean vs. scannable

Zebra striping and a colored left rule are both slightly "busier" than a pure-white table. Kept, because a front desk tracks a name across eight columns and scans down for the next flagged row — both cues do real work under time pressure. The compromise: striping is a 2-point-lightness tint, not a heavy band.

Judgment call

The benefit meter is the one piece of "data viz" allowed. It earns its place: "how much coverage is left" is the question staff ask most, and a bar answers it faster than reading two numbers and subtracting. Track and fill are solid (no gradient) so it prints. Everything else is plain label/value — a detail view is for reading exact figures, not for charts.

Why denser here

The report is read across a counter in seconds; the queue is worked head-down by one person. So the report gets 44px rows and 15px names, the queue gets 36px and 13px. Same tokens, different density — the type scale and color system don't change, only the rhythm does.

Confidence is deliberately grey

Parse confidence uses the neutral --ink-3 meter, not amber/red. It's a hint about the OCR/parse, not a clinical status — coloring it with status hues would dilute the one thing status color is allowed to mean. The amber chip, not the meter, is what flags a row for action.
